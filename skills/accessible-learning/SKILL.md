# Accessible Learning

Create and assess learning that is usable, respectful, relevant, and flexible for adult learners. Treat accessibility as a design requirement, not a final polish step.

## Choose the mode

Use **design mode** when planning, drafting, or revising material. Use **review mode** when assessing an existing artifact. Use both when revising after a review.

If essential context is missing, ask only for the audience, format, objective, and delivery setting. Otherwise state brief assumptions and proceed.

## Design mode

1. Identify the intended learners, their likely context, the learning goal, the delivery format, and meaningful constraints.
2. Align objectives, instruction, practice, feedback, and assessment. Prefer authentic, job- or life-relevant practice.
3. Build in purposeful options for engagement, representation, and action or expression. Do not add choices that obscure the goal or overload learners.
4. Use plain, respectful language; chunk content; introduce jargon only when needed; and connect new learning to existing experience.
5. Apply the format-specific guidance in `references/accessibility.md`.
6. End substantial work with a concise quality note: strengths, assumptions, and checks that require technical or learner validation.

## Review mode

1. Identify what is inspectable and name the limits of the review.
2. Read `references/review-rubric.md` and assess the artifact against the shared criteria plus its format-specific checks.
3. Report two to five strengths before findings.
4. Prioritize findings as **Critical**, **Important**, or **Enhancement**. For each finding, provide evidence, learner impact, and a specific revision.
5. Separate observable content issues from items that require testing in the source file, learning platform, or with assistive technology.
6. Offer a targeted revision when useful; do not rewrite the full artifact unless asked.

## Output format

1. **Snapshot** — artifact, intended learners, assumptions, and review limits.
2. **What works** — specific strengths.
3. **Priority findings** — impact and concrete revision for each.
4. **Quick wins** — immediate high-value edits.
5. **Technical or learner checks** — what remains to validate.
6. **Why it matters** — brief rationale. Provide sources only when requested or when using research-aware mode.

## Format routing

- For `.pptx`, slide decks, or presentations, use the Presentations skill to inspect and render the artifact. Read `references/accessibility.md` before reviewing.
- For `.docx` or Word materials, use the Documents skill and inspect both semantic structure and rendered appearance.
- For PDFs, use the PDF skill. Distinguish visible-content review from tagged-PDF and reading-order verification.
- For e-learning or web-delivered modules, inspect the visible flow and source where available; reserve keyboard, focus, caption, and screen-reader conformance claims for actual testing.

## Boundaries

- Do not call an artifact fully accessible or legally compliant from content review alone.
- Do not diagnose learners or prescribe individual accommodations.
- Do not assume a single learner pathway. Explain tradeoffs and preserve the author’s learning objective.
- Frame WCAG 2.2 and CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0 as foundations for practical guidance, not as a substitute for specialist review when formal conformance matters.
