# Inclusive Adult Learning Review Rubric

Use this rubric for adult-facing learning materials. Give priority to barriers that prevent access to the learning goal.

## Access and usability

Check whether learners can perceive, navigate, understand, and use the material with varied access needs. Look for clear structure, meaningful text alternatives, descriptive links, contrast and readable type, non-color cues, predictable navigation, and alternatives to audio-only, visual-only, or time-dependent interaction.

## Visual readability and legibility

Check whether type can be read comfortably in its intended setting. Assess font legibility, size in relation to screen size and viewing distance, contrast, line length, line spacing, density, and clear hierarchy. For slides, ask whether they will be projected, viewed on individual screens, shared virtually, or printed; do not judge size without that context.

For projected adult-learning decks, use **20pt as the default minimum for participant-facing text**. Treat 16–19pt participant-facing text as an Important finding and text below 16pt as Critical unless a clearly stated, tested viewing context supports it. Do not excuse small text because it is a label, citation, or footer: enlarge it, move nonessential detail to presenter notes or an accessible handout, or explicitly flag the residual barrier. Inspect actual text-object sizes and report the affected elements rather than relying only on a rendered visual impression.

Flag decorative or ambiguous type, text-heavy layouts, all-caps passages, and visual meaning conveyed only by color, position, or tiny labels.

## UDL

Check for purposeful options in engagement, representation, and action or expression. Do not call for every option in every artifact; judge whether the option is relevant to the barrier and goal.

## Adult learning

Check whether the experience respects adult learners' time and prior knowledge, makes the purpose clear, is problem-centered, gives opportunities for application, and supports transfer to real settings.

## Instructional alignment

Check whether objectives, instruction, examples, guided practice, independent practice, feedback, and assessment point to the same skill or knowledge. Flag hidden expectations and unclear directions.

## Inclusion and belonging

Check for respectful language, diverse and authentic examples, avoidance of stereotypes or deficit framing, and avoidable assumptions about culture, technology access, work, family, language, disability, or prior experience.

## Cognitive load

Check for one clear main idea at a time, useful hierarchy, meaningful signaling, manageable density, and sequencing that reduces unnecessary memory demands.

## Priority ratings

- **Critical:** blocks access to essential content, interaction, or assessment; or creates a serious exclusion risk.
- **Important:** materially impairs comprehension, participation, or transfer, even if a learner may work around it.
- **Enhancement:** improves clarity, flexibility, confidence, or polish without addressing a major barrier.

## Research-aware mode

When requested, attach a concise rationale or source for high-impact recommendations. Prefer authoritative guidance such as WCAG 2.2, CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0, and domain-appropriate learning-science sources. Do not manufacture citations or present theory as settled proof for a context it does not cover.
